Pilots are useful. They are also where most enterprise AI programs go to die—quietly, with a retrospective and a slide deck.

## What pilots actually prove

A pilot answers: *Can this work under controlled conditions?*  
It does not answer: *Will this repeat across thousands of practitioners under real governance, cost, and risk constraints?*

Confusing the two is how organizations celebrate local wins while enterprise metrics flatline.

## Systems, not heroics

An execution system has three non-negotiables:

- **A scoreboard** tied to workflow integration—not login counts or prompt volume.
- **A cadence** that forces diagnose → decide → execute on a fixed rhythm.
- **A feedback loop** from practitioners to leadership that changes priorities, not just status reports.

Hero projects don't scale. Systems do.

## The honest test

If you removed the champion team tomorrow, would adoption hold? Would metrics move without a re-launch campaign?

If not, you have a pilot—not a capability.
