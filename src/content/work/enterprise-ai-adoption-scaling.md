## Context

Aly Metwaly led AI adoption execution inside Nokia's product and engineering organization—thousands of practitioners across global sites, high regulatory and quality bar, and daily work anchored in specifications, code, and cross-team delivery.

Leadership had committed to AI. Usage was real but uneven: strong pockets of experimentation, weak repeatability, and no shared definition of what "adopted" meant outside individual team wins.

## Challenge

The organization did not lack tools or enthusiasm. It lacked an adoption system.

Pilots proved individual productivity gains but did not change how work flowed at scale. Teams could not answer three executive questions with confidence: who is actually using AI in production workflows, what changed in cycle time and quality, and what investment was required to sustain the gain.

Without that clarity, AI risked staying a side activity—valuable in demos, invisible in operating reviews.

## Approach

Built an adoption-first execution model—not a rollout deck, but a managed capability wired into how engineering work already happened.

- Defined adoption depth as workflow integration, not login counts: AI embedded in specification, coding, and review paths teams already owned.
- Established a single measurement spine so product, engineering, and leadership reviewed the same usage and output signals weekly.
- Paired enablement with accountability: team leads owned adoption targets; support structures removed friction instead of adding process.
- Sequenced for compounding wins—high-leverage workflows first, then expansion—so early gains funded organizational belief and the next wave of investment.

The operating principle: adoption changes how work gets done. Everything else is experimentation.

Usage moved from uneven experiments to organization-wide practice with defensible economics. Leadership could tie AI investment to hours recovered, output multiplied, and return on the execution effort—not narrative alone.
